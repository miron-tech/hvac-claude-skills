# Sales Coach Skill

> **Purpose:** When an estimate goes unsold, tell the contractor exactly what to say, when to say it, and why it works — so they close more jobs without spending another dollar on marketing.

---

## WHAT THIS SKILL DOES

- Analyzes conversation history (texts, emails, call notes) to identify customer stage and objections
- Classifies customer type (Emergency Buyer, Quote Shopper, Maintenance Upgrader, Skeptical Homeowner, Price-Sensitive, Referred Customer)
- Recommends exact follow-up message based on objection + customer type
- Provides **7-touch follow-up cadence** with specific timing and messaging for each touch
- Estimates **time-to-close** — how long this customer typically takes and how much follow-up effort is needed
- Teaches the contractor WHY each recommendation works (so they learn, not just copy-paste)
- Flags **dead lead signals** — when to stop following up
- Includes **maintenance plan pitch scripts** for post-repair and post-install upsell
- Includes **technician field scripts** — what the tech should say on-site to support the sale

## WHAT THIS SKILL DOES NOT DO

- Does not auto-send messages (contractor reviews and sends)
- Does not replace genuine customer relationships — it coaches, not manipulates
- Does not promise close rates — it improves them through structured follow-up
- Does not negotiate price for the contractor — it reframes value

## THE FOLLOW-UP GAP

Most HVAC contractors send one estimate and wait. The data says:
- **80% of sales require at least 5 follow-up contacts**
- Average buyer needs **7 touchpoints** before deciding
- Structured follow-up improves close rates from **25% to 40-50%**
- Going from 30% to 40% close rate = **33% revenue increase with zero extra marketing**

The #1 thing separating contractors who close at 50% from those at 25%: **they follow up.**

## CUSTOMER TYPE PROFILES

### Emergency Buyer
- **Situation:** System is dead. Needs fix NOW.
- **Psychology:** Speed and availability win. They'll pay more for "today."
- **Approach:** Don't oversell. Be available. Show up fast. The sale is yours to lose.
- **Close timeline:** Same day to 48 hours
- **Typical objection:** None (or "can you come sooner?")
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** After repair — "This could've been caught with a tune-up. $199/year and you never deal with this again."

### Quote Shopper
- **Situation:** Getting 3-5 estimates. Not in crisis. Comparing.
- **Psychology:** Wants to feel smart about their decision. Fears overpaying.
- **Approach:** Differentiate on value, not price. Ask what they're comparing. Provide a comparison framework that favors your strengths (warranty, rebates, reviews, speed).
- **Close timeline:** 3-14 days
- **Typical objection:** "I'm getting other quotes," "Your price is higher"
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** Include in the estimate — "This includes your first year of maintenance ($199 value)."

### Maintenance Upgrader
- **Situation:** System works but aging. Not urgent. Thinking ahead.
- **Psychology:** Analytical. Wants to do it right. Will research heavily.
- **Approach:** Long game. Plant seeds about efficiency, rebates, and "do it on your schedule vs. emergency." Give them resources, not pressure.
- **Close timeline:** 2-8 weeks (or seasonal — may wait for spring/fall)
- **Typical objection:** "We're not ready yet," "Maybe next year"
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** "Start with a maintenance plan. We'll tune up what you have and give you a real assessment of how much life is left. When you're ready to replace, you'll already be our priority customer."

### Skeptical Homeowner
- **Situation:** Had a bad experience with a contractor before. Doesn't trust the industry.
- **Psychology:** Defensive. Assumes you're upselling. Needs proof.
- **Approach:** Lead with reviews, certifications, licenses, warranties. Offer attorney/advisor review of contract. Be radically transparent.
- **Close timeline:** 1-4 weeks
- **Typical objection:** "How do I know you're legit?" "Last contractor overcharged me"
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** Don't pitch on first interaction. Build trust first. Offer it after a successful repair where they saw your work.

### Price-Sensitive
- **Situation:** Budget is tight. May have been shocked by replacement cost.
- **Psychology:** Wants to say yes but can't see how to afford it. Embarrassed to say "I can't afford it."
- **Approach:** Financing is the answer, NOT discounting. Show monthly cost, not total cost. Show net monthly cost after energy savings. Rebates are your best friend here.
- **Close timeline:** 1-2 weeks (once financing removes the barrier)
- **Typical objection:** "It's too expensive," "I need to think about it" (usually means budget)
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** Offer monthly payment ($17/month sounds different than $199/year)

### Referred Customer
- **Situation:** Friend/neighbor/family member recommended you. Highest trust from the start.
- **Psychology:** Already believes you're good. Wants to validate the referral.
- **Approach:** Lean into the connection. "Your neighbor was great to work with." Social proof is already done — go for the close faster.
- **Close timeline:** 1-7 days (fastest average close)
- **Typical objection:** Rarely price — usually timing
- **Maintenance plan pitch:** "Your neighbor [name] is on our maintenance plan. They love it."

## 7-TOUCH FOLLOW-UP CADENCE

**For unsold estimates (any customer type):**

| Touch | Timing | Channel | Message Focus |
|-------|--------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | Day 0 | In-person / phone | Present estimate. Walk through options. Answer questions. |
| 2 | Day 1 | Text | "Thanks for your time yesterday. Let me know if any questions come up." |
| 3 | Day 3 | Text or email | Specific value-add: rebate reminder, energy savings calc, or seasonal urgency |
| 4 | Day 7 | Phone call | "Checking in on your decision. Anything I can help with?" |
| 5 | Day 10 | Text | New angle: testimonial from a similar customer, or "your neighbor just installed the same system" |
| 6 | Day 14 | Email | "Estimate recap" — resend the key numbers with a fresh perspective |
| 7 | Day 21 | Text | Gentle close: "Still thinking about the AC? I've got some availability [this week] if you want to move forward." |

**After touch 7 with no engagement:** Move to **monthly drip** (seasonal reminder, energy tip, or rebate update). Don't keep weekly follow-up on a dead lead.

**Adjust cadence by customer type:**
```
IF Emergency Buyer → Compress to touches 1-3 only (same day, next day, day 3). They decide fast.
IF Quote Shopper → Full 7-touch. They need comparison time.
IF Maintenance Upgrader → Extend timing: Day 0, Day 7, Day 14, Day 30, Day 45, Day 60, Day 90.
IF Referred Customer → Compress to touches 1-4. They close fast or they won't.
IF Price-Sensitive → Add a financing-specific touch at Day 2: "Did you know this comes out to $X/month?"
```

## OBJECTION RESPONSE PLAYBOOK

### "It's too expensive"

**Wrong:** "That's our best price." (Dead end)
**Wrong:** "I can take $500 off." (Race to the bottom — you just trained them to ask for more)

**Right — The Monthly Cost Reframe:**
"I hear you. Let me show you what it actually costs per month. The Better system at $9,200 is $107/month financed. But your new system saves about $71/month in energy costs. So your real cost is $36/month — less than your phone bill. And unlike your phone, this system pays you back."

**Right — The Comparison Reframe (for quote shoppers):**
"What are you comparing it to? I'd love to see the other quotes — not to match the price, but to make sure you're comparing the same thing. Equipment brand, warranty length, SEER rating, who's pulling the permit, what's included in labor. A $6,500 quote that skips permits and uses builder-grade equipment ends up costing you more in 3-5 years."

### "I want to get other quotes"

**Wrong:** "No problem, take your time." (You lose)
**Wrong:** "I'll beat any quote." (Commodity trap)

**Right — The Comparison Framework:**
"You absolutely should. Here's what I'd ask every contractor:
1. What brand and model are they quoting? (SEER2 rating?)
2. What's the warranty — parts AND labor?
3. Are they pulling a permit and scheduling inspection?
4. Will they handle the rebate paperwork?
5. How many installations of this exact system have they done?

I can answer all five of those right now. And whatever you decide, I'm happy to walk through the other quotes with you to compare apples to apples."

### "We don't need it right now"

**Wrong:** "You're right, it's fine." (Siding with inaction)
**Wrong:** "Your system could die any day!" (Fear-mongering)

**Right — The Cost of Waiting:**
"That's fair — you're not in a crisis. But here's what I'd want you to know: your current system is costing about $100/month more in energy than a new one. Plus there's about $125/month in emergency repair risk at 16 years old. That's $225/month — in 6 months, that's $1,350 toward the new system that you just... spent.

Also — the $1,500 TVA rebate? Those programs don't last forever. I'd hate for you to miss out on $4,100 in rebates because you waited a season."

### "The price is higher than the other quote"

**Right — The Value Stack:**
"Can I ask what brand and SEER rating they quoted? And what warranty?

[If they share details:]
So they quoted a [brand] at SEER2 [X] with a [Y]-year warranty. Here's the difference:
- My system saves $[X] more per year in energy
- My warranty covers [Y more years]
- We pull the permit and schedule inspection (not all contractors do)
- We handle all the rebate paperwork (that's $[X] you'd lose if it's filed wrong)
- Over 15 years, the 'cheaper' system actually costs $[X] more to own.

I'm not the cheapest quote you'll get. But I might be the most valuable one."

### "Let me talk to my spouse"

**Right:**
"Absolutely — big decision. Would it help if I put together a one-page summary with the three options and the financing math? That way you can walk through it together without trying to remember everything. I can text it to you in 10 minutes.

What day works for me to follow up? I want to make sure all their questions get answered too."

### "I've had bad experiences with contractors"

**Right:**
"I hear that a lot, honestly. And I'm sorry that happened. Here's what I'd offer:
- Look us up — we've got [X] reviews at [X] stars on Google
- I'll send you the contract in advance so you can review it on your own time — or have your attorney look at it
- We close through our payment processor — your payment is held until the job is complete and you're satisfied
- I can connect you with a homeowner we just did this exact system for if you want a reference.

I'd rather earn your trust than ask for it."

## TECHNICIAN FIELD SCRIPTS

The tech on-site has the most trust. Here's what they should say:

### After a Repair (Maintenance Plan Pitch)
"Good news — I got your [problem] fixed. Everything's running now. One thing I noticed though — your system is about [age] years old, and I saw [specific wear sign]. A lot of homeowners with systems this age end up on our maintenance plan. It's $199 a year — two tune-ups, priority scheduling, and 15% off parts. Basically, we catch stuff like this before it turns into an emergency. Want me to have [office person/owner] set that up for you?"

### After a Repair on an Old System (Replacement Seed)
"I fixed the [problem] and you're good for now. But I want to be straight with you — your system is [age] years old, and I noticed [specific issue]. This repair was $[X], and with a system this age, it's not uncommon to see repairs like this once or twice a year. At some point the math tips toward replacement. If you want, I can have [owner] put together what that would look like — no pressure, just so you have the numbers."

### During a Tune-Up (Replacement Conversation)
"Everything looks okay today, but your system is [age] years old and the efficiency is down from where it was new. You're probably paying $[X] more per year in energy than you would with a new system. I'm not saying you need to replace it now — but when you're ready, there are some great rebates available that could save you $[X]. Want me to have [owner] run some numbers?"

## DEAD LEAD SIGNALS

Stop following up when you see:

| Signal | What It Means | Action |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| No response to 4+ messages | They've decided (probably with someone else) | One final "door open" text, then monthly drip |
| "We went with someone else" | Lost deal | Ask: "Totally fair. Mind if I ask what made the difference? Helps me improve." Then stop. |
| "We're going to wait" + 30 days | They're not buying this season | Move to seasonal drip list |
| Blocked your number / unsubscribed | Clear rejection | Remove from active follow-up immediately |
| Combative responses | "Stop texting me," hostility | Stop immediately. One polite close: "Understood. Sorry for the trouble." |
| Estimate was 60+ days ago | Stale beyond recovery | One re-engagement attempt with fresh angle, then drip |

**The honest truth:** Not every estimate will close. A contractor at 50% close rate — which is top-tier — still loses half their estimates. The goal is to lose them after 7 touches, not after 1.

## DECISION LOGIC

```
IF no response for 3-5 days after estimate sent →
  Send Touch 3 (value-add, not "just following up")
  Never say "just following up" — it adds no value

IF customer said "too expensive" →
  Monthly cost reframe + financing. If that fails, Cost of Waiting.

IF customer said "getting other quotes" →
  Comparison framework. Position yourself to be the LAST call, not the first.

IF customer said "not ready yet" →
  Cost of Waiting + seasonal urgency + rebate expiration

IF customer said "let me talk to my spouse" →
  Provide one-page summary. Set specific follow-up date.

IF customer was a repair call and didn't get maintenance plan →
  Tech should pitch on-site. If missed, send text: "Hope the AC is treating you well!
  Just wanted to mention our maintenance plan — $17/month catches issues before they
  become emergencies. Want me to set that up?"

IF referred customer hasn't decided in 7 days →
  Something is off. Call (don't text) and ask directly.

IF emergency buyer hasn't booked in 24 hours →
  They probably already booked someone else. One more call: "Hey, wanted to make sure
  you got taken care of. Did you get the AC fixed?"

IF estimate is 14+ days old with no engagement →
  Switch angle entirely. New information: seasonal rebate, competitor comparison,
  neighbor testimonial, or energy savings update.

IF customer asks about competitor by name →
  Never trash them. "They're a solid company. Here's what's different about us: [specifics]."

DEFAULT → Follow the 7-touch cadence. Don't skip touches. Don't give up at touch 2.
```

- [ ] Never recommend discounting — reframe value instead

She replied on Day 1 saying "thanks, let me look it over" and hasn't responded since. I know she's getting at least 2 other quotes. What do I say?

### Example Output:

---

## SALES COACHING
**Customer:** Sarah | **Address:** 3847 Oakwood Dr, Memphis 38128
**Estimate:** $9,200 (Better tier, Carrier 4-ton 16 SEER2) | **Sent:** 5 days ago
**Customer Type:** Quote Shopper | **Stage:** Evaluation (comparing)

### WHAT I'M READING

**Motivation: MODERATE-HIGH**
- Her system failed (emergency origin) — she needs a solution
- She's actively getting quotes — she's buying, just deciding from whom
- "Let me look it over" on Day 1 = polite, engaged, not rejecting

**Likely Situation Right Now:**
- She has 2-3 quotes and is trying to compare them
- She probably doesn't know how to compare (SEER, warranty, brand quality all look different)
- She's waiting for the last quote to come in before she decides
- 5 days of silence = she hasn't decided yet. That's GOOD. If she picked someone else, she'd usually ghost or say so.

**Time-to-Close Estimate:** Quote shoppers average 5-14 days. You're at Day 5 — right in the window. Expect 2-3 more touches over the next 7 days. Total follow-up time: ~20 minutes. At a $9,200 ticket, that's $460+/hour return on your time.

### YOUR NEXT MESSAGE (Text):

"Hey Sarah — I know you're comparing options on the AC replacement. One thing that's worth looking at: the federal tax credit on the system I quoted you is $600, plus your new system saves about $850/year in energy costs. Those numbers change the total cost picture pretty significantly.

Happy to walk through a side-by-side comparison if you have other quotes to look at. No pressure — just want to make sure you're comparing the full picture."

### WHY THIS WORKS:

1. **Acknowledges she's comparing** without being needy about it
2. **Adds new information** (rebate + energy savings) — not "just following up"
3. **Offers to compare quotes for her** — positions you as the advisor, not the salesman
4. **"No pressure"** matches her pace — she's analytical, she hates being pushed
5. **Specific numbers** ($600, $850/yr) make the value concrete, not abstract

### WHAT TO EXPECT:

**"Can you send me a comparison of what's included?"**
→ Great. Send a one-page comparison: your quote vs. what to ask the other contractors. Use the 5-question comparison framework from the objection playbook.

**"I got a quote for $6,500 from [competitor]"**
→ "What brand and warranty are they quoting? I'd love to compare apples to apples — a $2,700 gap usually means different equipment, warranty, or scope. Let me show you what's different."

**[No response for 3 more days]**
→ Touch 4 (Day 8): Call. "Hey Sarah, [Name] from [Company]. Just wanted to check in on the AC decision. Any questions I can answer?" Phone call at this stage feels natural, not pushy.

### WHAT NOT TO SAY:

- "Just checking in" — adds zero value
- "Did you get a chance to look at the estimate?" — implies she's been lazy
- "I can match that price" — never race to the bottom
- "Our slots are filling up" — fake urgency on a $9K purchase backfires
- "What can I do to earn your business?" — desperate, gives her all the power
